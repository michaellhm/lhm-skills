# Anti-AI design rules

These came from real client feedback ("it looks very AI"). Each one names the tell, why it reads as generated, and what to do instead.

## Layout and decoration

- **Small uppercase labels above every heading** ("WHAT WE DO", "HOW YOU SEE US"). Generated pages stack these on every section. Use at most one, in the hero, if it adds information.
- **Numbered cards (01, 02, 03).** Decorative numbering implies a sequence that isn't there. Drop it.
- **Floating stat cards over the hero image** ("260+ reviews" badge) and **rows of tick-mark trust claims under the buttons.** Both feel templated. Keep the hero to a headline, one line of copy, two buttons and real media.
- **Hero background that stops halfway down.** If the hero has a background colour, run it the full height of the section.
- **Big stats band** (four large numbers in a dark strip). Remove it unless the client asks; put credible figures inside sections where they support a point.
- **Comparison boxes and explainer widgets** the user didn't ask for (for example "Physio or EP?"). Cut them; they make the page longer and more generic.
- **FAQ accordions on the homepage.** They belong on an FAQ page.
- **Dark "special" card in a row of light cards** (for example a dark referrer card among patient cards). It pulls focus. Keep cards consistent and mark the difference with a small accent colour.
- **Rounded card floating on white for the final call to action.** Use a full-width band.

## Copy

- **Clever taglines** ("Three disciplines, one coordinated team", "Three ways we care, one standard of care"). Use plain headings: "Our services", "How you can access our services", "Conditions we treat", "What our clients say", "Our clinics".
- **Triplets** ("Lower impact, easier movement, real progress"). Vary the rhythm and say something specific instead.
- **Unprovable warmth** ("clinicians who know their clients by name"). Replace with a factual line.
- **Em dashes**, US spelling, and words like "seamless", "robust" or "elevate". Follow the anti-AI writing guidelines.
- **Invented facts.** No made-up response times, statistics, awards or review scores. Quote reviews verbatim and trim with an ellipsis rather than rewording.

## Colour and type

- **Generic AI palettes** (teal plus mint, purple gradients). Use the client's own colours from their live site and footer.
- **Bright brand green with white text.** It usually fails contrast. Use a darker shade of the brand colour for text-bearing surfaces and keep the bright shade for accents, or use dark text on it.
- **Heavy weights and huge display type everywhere.** Headings at 500 to 600 weight read as established and calm.

## Imagery

- **Stock-looking or low-resolution images stretched large.** Search the client's service, location and team pages for better originals. Crop out logos and badges baked into photos with a slight zoom.
- **Phone mock-ups or app screenshots** for a service like telehealth. Use a real photo (a laptop on a video call worked better).
- **Background video with burned-in captions or title cards.** Pick a clean stretch, loop it, and position the video so captions fall outside the frame. Add a pause control and respect reduced motion. Recommend a short caption-free shoot.

## What adds polish without looking generated

- Photo-led service cards with a thin brand-colour line and a gentle hover lift.
- A dark band for one dense section (conditions as tabs with a matching photo).
- A short brand-colour rule above section headings.
- Real review cards with stars and the review platform name.
- Carousels for genuinely large sets (awards by year, the team in one row), with arrows, keyboard support and no autoplay.
- Gentle fade-in on scroll (under 500ms, off for reduced motion).
- The real team cut-out breaking out of the top of the final call-to-action band.
