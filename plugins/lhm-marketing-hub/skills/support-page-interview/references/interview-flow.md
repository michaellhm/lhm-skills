# Interview flow

Use the supplied reference page to understand its sequence, tone and role. Extract structure, not unsupported practice facts. If browsing is unavailable, use the bundled snapshot and state that you have not checked the current online version. Adapt to the chosen support topic and audience rather than mechanically replacing the word “child”.

Suggested progression:
1. **Scope:** who this page is for, ages/concerns included, exclusions, and which services are genuinely available. Distinguish therapy, assessment and professional supervision.
2. **What brings people here:** everyday concerns and how the person describes them; parents’ observations and the child’s experience may differ. Ask for general examples, never real case histories.
3. **What they could work towards:** realistic, individual goals and everyday changes. What varies? Avoid guarantees, cure claims and fixed recovery timelines.
4. **Making sense of it:** contributing factors, misconceptions and the explanation the clinician wishes people heard early. Do not diagnose the reader.
5. **Approach:** what actually happens, how the approach is chosen, parent/carer involvement, consent and information-sharing where relevant. Do not infer that every independent clinician works the same way.
6. **When/where to seek advice:** when another professional or service may be more appropriate. Collect approved practice wording; never invent crisis service details or turn this into triage.
7. **FAQs:** ask what people really ask. Explore first appointment, preparation, referrals/funding, costs, therapy versus assessment and session planning as relevant. Collect confirmed answers, flag admin facts for the practice manager.
8. **Helpful information:** resources the clinician recommends and why. Label unverified links; do not imply endorsements or reproduce patient material.
9. **Next step:** how the practice wants enquiries handled, clinician matching, confirmed locations and service links. Availability and funding vary; do not invent them.

Before concluding, ask what sounds unlike the clinician, what is missing, and what claims need softening. Summarise into the same page file. Practical details may remain “Needs confirmation”; ask whether the brief is accurate rather than demanding answers they cannot provide.
