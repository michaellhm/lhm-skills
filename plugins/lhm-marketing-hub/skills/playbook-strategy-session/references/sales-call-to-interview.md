# From sales call to website-content interview

Use when a sales conversation is the starting point for a client onboarding, business-brain or website strategy interview. Preserve this reusable flow while tailoring invitations and follow-ups to the client; do not merely substitute names into a fixed questionnaire.

## 1. Read the sales conversation as evidence

Read the available full transcript, summary, accepted written scope and subsequent correspondence. Build a compact source ledger:

| Category | Capture | How it changes preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client-reported facts | Services actually delivered, current capacity, booking process, existing relationships | Continue from the fact; clarify current detail rather than ask it again |
| Beliefs and preferences | Care philosophy, own phrases, tone, desired independence | Explore meaning and concrete examples |
| Goals and future ideas | Current success definition versus later expansion/offers | Keep horizons distinct; relative time is not a fixed date |
| Seller examples and hypotheses | Suggested audiences, services, page architecture or channels | Do not turn illustrations into client facts |
| Promises and expectations | Deliverables, integrations, training, support, costs and follow-up | Internal reconciliation against accepted scope; not automatic approved scope |
| Conflicts and gaps | Stale website hours, mixed speaker labels, unresolved questions | Ask the smallest useful clarification and retain source/timestamp |

Attribute quotations only when speaker identity is reliable. Use a summary as secondary evidence when the transcript differs. Acceptance does not verify payment; a promised capability does not mean access or functionality is verified. Do not repeat sales assertions about search rankings, AI recommendations or technical implementation as guarantees.

Prepare a short “You mentioned X; could you tell me more about Y?” continuation list with source timestamps. Keep this separate from the interview's broad invitations. Focus on information needed for the website; this is not a new sales pitch or a technical audit.

## 2. Default seven-theme flow

Use the sequence below for an approximately one-hour call. Adapt language to patients/customers and industry. Suggested allocation: opening 2 minutes; themes 1–7: 7, 7, 6, 12, 7, 12 and 5 minutes; 2 minutes flexibility. Preserve the client's hard stop and closing read-back. Prompts are optional; do not squeeze every prompt into the hour.

### 1 — Story, purpose and future

**Invitation:** Why did you create this business, and what did you want it to stand for?

Follow the origin into purpose, values and future working life. Explore what should stay true as it grows and how current priorities differ from future aspirations. Neutral territories: a formative experience, a gap they wanted to address, the kind of business they wanted to run.

**Listen for / website use:** Founder language, About/team pages, purpose and long-term brand direction. Do not force a polished mission statement live.

### 2 — Philosophy: common problems and misconceptions

**Invitation:** What problems do people most often bring to you, and what do you wish they understood about those problems?

Ask separately: What do people believe is happening? What are they worried about? What advice or assumptions do you often need to unpack? What needs nuance rather than a simple correction? How do you explain your view in plain language? What do you consider your role and theirs? What would you never promise?

Neutral territories depend on the business: expectations about process, timescale, effort or common advice. Do not assert that a particular belief is wrong for everyone.

**Listen for / website use:** Core beliefs, misconceptions paired with the client's explanation, approach copy, condition/service introductions and FAQs. Keep this about beliefs and understanding; practical delivery comes in theme 4.

### 3 — People they help best

**Invitation:** Who do you work especially well with, and what is happening in their life when they come to you?

If the sales call mentioned a specific but undefined ideal customer, continue from that statement. Ask what they want back, the words they use, their concerns, appropriate fit and referral boundaries, and which groups should lead the website. Prioritising a group need not exclude other suitable customers. Do not equate barriers such as fear, disability, cost or time with poor character or motivation.

**Listen for / website use:** Audience hierarchy, patient/customer psychology, homepage angle and service pathways. Seller-generated example audiences are not selected audiences.

### 4 — How they help, what is distinctive and concrete stories

**Invitation:** Choose one common problem and walk me through how you help, from first understanding it to the person's next step.

Use two passes:

- **Understand the problem:** Impact on life, previous attempts, what the practitioner/provider needs to know, reasoning and boundaries.
- **Explain the approach:** Assessment, explanation, actual services, why this plan, adaptation, progress and what changes next.

Use one or two de-identified stories to reveal the approach. Ask about the observed change, limitations and what the case illustrates; do not require dramatic success. Stories supplied for internal research are not automatically approved public case studies or testimonials.

Find the positioning explicitly: “If someone is choosing between you and another provider, what should help them decide whether you're right for them?” Ask what they would actually experience, which differences are personal practice versus the profession in general, and what evidence supports the distinction. Where relevant, ask how they explain choosing between modalities and when another practitioner or complementary approach is appropriate. Avoid prompting unsupported superiority or blanket criticism.

**Listen for / website use:** Why choose us, process, problem-to-approach examples, service copy, differentiators and support needed for claims. Do not preselect a marketing angle before hearing the answers.

### 5 — Becoming a patient/customer

**Invitation:** What should someone understand before booking, and what can they expect when they first work with you?

Clarify the real first-contact/booking route, initial service, what they leave with, follow-ups, classes or other pathways, common questions and relevant practical details. If a discovery/fit call was not established, ask whether it exists rather than assuming it. Capture current capacity and items such as fees/funding that need factual confirmation. Keep routine access and billing administration for follow-up.

**Listen for / website use:** Getting started, booking calls to action, offer clarity and practical FAQs.

### 6 — Voice and website, including ten sentences

First ask openly what the website should sound like, how it should make people feel and what it should never sound like. Continue from any written tone brief; do not request it again. Invite a short spoken explanation of their approach before offering examples.

Then generate **ten client-specific sentence probes**. Deliberately vary directness, warmth, jargon, responsibility, performance or lifestyle focus, relationship style and familiar marketing language. Include plausible near-misses and contrasts, rather than ten obvious affirmations or only cartoonishly bad examples. Do not show predicted answers or label rows as “good/bad”. The task is to discover preferences, not obtain approval of agent-written copy.

Say: “Mark yes, no or unsure for whether this sounds like you and belongs on your website. If it's nearly right, tell us what you'd change.”

| # | Sentence | Yes / No / Unsure | What would you change? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–10 | One tailored sentence per row in the generated pack | | |

Ask why a no/unsure feels wrong, one follow-up at a time. Preserve ratings, reasons and exact replacements. A table of all ten supports scanning; work through rows individually if that suits voice mode. If time is short, collect quick ratings, then revisit the most revealing responses. Finish by asking for one sentence they would actually say and one they would never say.

A tone preference is separate from factual accuracy, clinical substantiation and final copy approval. Prefer contrasts that do not require unsafe or misleading wording. If a supplied or deliberately tested sentence contains a guarantee or unsupported claim, label it internally as unsuitable for publication, even if the client likes its tone. For different founders, retain differences and unresolved consensus rather than invent agreement.

**Listen for / website use:** Voice guide, vocabulary, words to avoid, headline directions and sample copy for later review. Keep another client's answers and details out of this client's pack.

### 7 — Angle, success and read-back

Reflect an evidenced provisional synthesis: “You help [people] with [problem/goal] through [specific approach], so they can [meaningful aim]. What have we missed or misunderstood?” Do not fill placeholders with invented answers. Test whether this explains why someone would choose them.

Confirm priorities now versus later, realistic success/capacity, first website priorities, decision-makers, open questions and agreed next review. Record owners and dates only when established. Do not let hosting or access setup consume the close.

**Listen for / website use:** Positioning direction for confirmation, launch emphasis, approval path and remaining copy gaps.

## 3. Website coverage and reusable outputs

Alongside playbook coverage, map evidence to: homepage audience/problem/promise/angle; About/team/story/credentials; approach and difference; current services/conditions; FAQ misconceptions/concerns; getting-started and booking; voice and sentence replacements; and any necessary factual/claims checks. Mark each complete, partial or missing; this interview may not establish every service-page detail. Do not invent medical facts or finished copy to fill gaps.

Output the tailored meeting pack and coach prompt under the verified work root. Include the source ledger, continuation list and internal scope questions in the pack. If client context setup is authorised, save concise, sourced canonical Obsidian facts and the verified work links; do not copy whole deliverables into the vault. Mark preparation as preparation and leave the interview/handover pending until evidenced.

After the interview, produce the source brief with quotes, problem-to-approach pairs, differentiators/support/limits, audience hierarchy, sentence ratings/replacements, provisional angle, page coverage, open checks and agreed next steps. The campaign-playbook generator owns the finished playbook. A deferred sales handover stays deferred until the user resumes it.
