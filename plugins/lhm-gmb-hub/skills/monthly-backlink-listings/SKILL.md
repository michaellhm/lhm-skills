# Monthly backlink listings

## Outcome and authority

Build useful, accurate business listings and relevant public resources. Aim for 2–5 verified new placements per client per calendar month; five is a hard cap, two is a target, never a quota. Prefer fewer high-quality placements over irrelevant profiles. A listing is valuable for business discovery, referrals and accurate entity information; do not promise rankings or chase followed links as the sole objective.

Treat an explicit recurring registration instruction as continuing authority for free, accurate registrations for that named business once its approved email and credential storage are verified. Do not repeatedly ask approval for each routine authorised registration. Client rollout needs that client's scope and authorisation; the LHM pilot does not authorise every client. Paid plans, memberships, reciprocal-link obligations, outreach messages, mailbox creation, identity verification, deployment and broader account permissions need their own authority. Honour site terms; do not bypass CAPTCHA, anti-bot controls or identity checks. Continue independent research when registration is blocked.

## Resolve context

1. Read the plugin's `references/obsidian-context-contract.md`. Resolve the active shared LHM Knowledge vault and verified work root separately. Internal LHM work uses its internal workspace, not a fabricated client folder. Read applicable vault governance. Missing governance records are reported; do not invent a migration or write to both old and new vaults.
2. Load the canonical business record, website, industry, country/service area, public name/address/phone, existing citations, truthful description, owned media and existing account register. Verify website redirects/canonical URL before submission. Do not invent addresses, professional credentials, testimonials or branch locations. Apply health-sector/AHPRA content rules when relevant.
3. Load the knowledge candidate inventory and per-client programme configuration. The bundled `references/source-candidates.csv` is a dated seed, never proof of availability or link type. Use assets as schemas; private run state never belongs in the plugin repository.
4. Verify the registration mailbox is accessible, owner-approved and permitted for public disclosure where required. Prefer a role mailbox controlled by the business. For clients, prefer a client-owned mailbox or documented managed alias with handover rights. Passwords, recovery codes, tokens, verification links and MFA secrets stay in the approved password manager; knowledge stores only an opaque item reference and recovery owner. Do not create accounts unless credentials can be saved securely and verified. Never print secrets or place them in tool output, Git, reports or shared vaults.

## Monthly procedure

1. Acquire a per-client run lock/checkpoint in the verified work root; use local timezone and a `client + YYYY-MM` run key. Read all previous entries, including pending verification and unknown submission outcomes. An active lock means stop duplicate execution. Resolve stale locks from saved evidence; never blindly repeat writes. Count already-live new placements for this calendar month before calculating remaining allowance. Each new submission or unresolved attempt consumes an allowance slot until reconciled; total newly initiated submissions cannot exceed five in a month.
2. Refresh the Medium source and compare normalised registrable domains/paths against the saved inventory. Capture additions, removals and dates; unavailable sources retain the last snapshot with an explicit refresh failure. Do not copy the full article. Keep provenance and observed facts distinct from author claims.
3. Check existing placements/accounts before finding new ones. Search the platform for business name, domain and known profile URLs. Claim/update an existing listing rather than create a duplicate, if authorised. Recheck prior pending outcomes first. Do not retry an uncertain submission until reconciliation proves it absent.
4. Screen candidates on current live evidence: relevant audience/industry or geography, legitimate business eligibility, useful content, free public listing option, terms-compatible access, no forced reciprocal links, no deceptive/irrelevant category and no obvious spam network. Review the article's general business directory candidates first, then discover industry/local equivalents using official industry bodies, chambers, professional directories, partner listings and relevant competitor citations. Membership-only opportunities are research items until membership/budget authority exists. Reject unrelated musician, hobby, aviation or software identities for LHM; another client may have a genuine reason to use them.
5. Rank candidates by relevance, legitimate discovery/referral value, confidence and effort. Record the evidence URL, date and accept/reject reason. Check an example public profile where available: link destination, `rel` values, redirects, robots/noindex and public access. Record followed/nofollow/ugc/sponsored/unknown; never label a source claim as verified. Indexability is an observation, not proof Google indexed a page; absence of noindex does not guarantee indexing. A nofollow listing can still be useful.
6. Select at most the remaining allowance. Prepare accurate, platform-appropriate descriptions and owned media. Use the business name or plain URL as natural anchor text. Avoid thin content, fake reviews, generic forum signatures, keyword stuffing and low-quality bookmark pages. Small monthly volume does not make link spam safe. Follow Google's current spam policies: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam.
7. For each eligible candidate, save a prepared checkpoint, then use an available supported connector or observed browser form. Save the account in the password manager before completion. Verify email only through authorised mailbox access. Pause that candidate for MFA/CAPTCHA/human verification or inaccessible credential tooling; record the specific next action. Never claim unattended account registration works until a pilot has demonstrated the full path. No repeated confirmations for already-authorised routine actions.
8. Immediately record submission outcome, account reference, pending moderation and public profile URL. Read back the public page and verify it identifies the correct business and links to the intended website. Only public verified placements count as live; submitted/pending/blocked are separate. Persist after each attempt so interruption cannot create duplicates. Release the run lock on clean completion; preserve checkpoint on failure.
9. Save a dated report under `monthly-backlink-listings/YYYY-MM/` in the verified work root. Update shared knowledge with concise programme status and account references, and keep detailed history in the work register. Include source changes, screened/rejected/new opportunities, live links, link attributes, pending outcomes, allowance usage, blockers/owners and next month's shortlist. Recheck recent links for removal/attribute changes; only report indexing or referral results when evidenced.

## Schedule and pilot

When scheduling is requested, inspect existing automations and update a matching one instead of duplicating it. Default: a heartbeat in the current chat, first day of each month at 09:00 Australia/Melbourne. Save exact knowledge/work/config paths, registration scope, month cap, source and durable resume checkpoint in its prompt. Do not assume installed skill access: pin the reviewed source or use the saved SOP, state deployment status explicitly. Notify on a completed run, meaningful change, failure or required user action; stay quiet on unchanged non-actionable state.

Run an initial read-only pilot immediately: source extraction/diff, candidate screening, duplicate and context checks, output readback. Attempt one real eligible registration only after mailbox, secure credential persistence and business details are verified. Report research-only versus registration-tested precisely. Missing mailbox must not prevent scheduled research. Roll out to each client from `assets/programme-template.md` with separate scope, account ownership, monthly budget, config/register, duplicate schedule check and pilot.

## Acceptance examples

- A music-only profile for an agency: reject even if followed.
- Four submissions this month and one unknown outcome: no new submission until reconciled; never create a sixth.
- Existing profile: reuse/claim if authorised, do not create a second.
- No accessible mailbox/password manager: research continues, registration is blocked, secrets remain outside knowledge.
- Author claims dofollow but observed link has `rel=ugc`: record ugc and evaluate actual business value.
- Moderated submission: pending, not live; resolve before retrying.
- Another client's unapproved schedule: prepare the template, do not enrol automatically.
